## Context

What forced a choice. The constraints that were real, including the ones that
are about the organization rather than the technology — who has to operate this,
what they already know, what happens when they are on vacation.

## Decision

What was chosen, stated plainly enough that someone can disagree with it.

## Alternatives

One subsection per alternative that was genuinely considered. Each one states
what it would have been better at, because an ADR that makes the rejected
options look foolish is not recording a decision — it is defending one, and it
will not help the person who has to revisit it in two years.

## Consequences

What this makes easy, what it makes hard, and what it forecloses. Include the
costs that only appear later: operational burden, the failure mode nobody sees
until it happens, and the migration cost if this turns out to be wrong.

## Revisit when

The specific condition that should reopen this. "If X ships support for Y" or
"above N employees" — something observable, so this does not have to be
re-litigated on a hunch.
